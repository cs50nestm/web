## Overview

Generative AI is one of the most significant — and contested — technological forces of our time. In this Changemaker Project, you will research how it works, analyze its real-world impacts, compare how different countries are regulating it, and propose your own recommendations for responsible use. You are not just studying this issue — you are developing a position on it and making a case for change.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this project, you will be able to:

- Explain how generative AI works at a conceptual level
- Analyze the economic, social, and ethical implications of generative AI
- Compare AI regulatory approaches across multiple countries
- Construct and defend an original, evidence-based policy recommendation
- Present your findings clearly and persuasively

---

## What Your Project Must Include

Your deliverable — website or Google Slides — must address all four parts below.

### Part 1: How Generative AI Works

Explain generative AI clearly for a general audience. Cover:

- What it is, with examples (ChatGPT, DALL·E, Midjourney, GitHub Copilot, etc.)
- How these models are trained: datasets, neural networks, large language models
- What transformers and prompt engineering are, at a conceptual level
- At least one visual (diagram or infographic) illustrating the process

### Part 2: Costs, Benefits & Ethical Concerns

Analyze the broader impact of generative AI. Address **at least three** of the following:

- Economic impact: job changes, productivity, new industries
- Education: benefits and risks, academic integrity, access gaps
- Ethics: bias, copyright and IP questions, misinformation and deepfakes
- Environment: energy and resource costs of training large models

Include a **visual pros/cons table or infographic** summarizing your analysis.

### Part 3: Global Regulation Comparison

Research and compare how **at least three** of these regions approach AI regulation:

- **United States** — executive orders, proposed legislation, industry self-regulation
- **European Union** — the EU AI Act: risk tiers, banned uses, compliance rules
- **China** — state content controls and licensing requirements
- **Canada** — the Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA)

Create a **comparison chart or table** showing key differences across the regions you chose.

### Part 4: Your Policy Recommendation

This is the heart of your Changemaker Project. Draft an original recommendation that addresses:

- What — if anything — should the U.S. do to regulate generative AI?
- How should AI be handled in education, business, or creative industries?
- How do you balance innovation with safety and fairness?

Your recommendation must **take a clear position**, back it up with evidence from your research, and **acknowledge the strongest counterargument**.

## Source Requirements

- Minimum **5 credible sources**
- At least **1 official or primary source** (e.g., EU AI Act text, a government report or executive order)
- At least **1 expert or journalistic source** (think tank report, MIT Tech Review, Wired, AP, Reuters, etc.)
- Wikipedia may help you find sources — it is not a citable source itself
- All sources cited in **MLA or APA format** in your works cited

## Extension Option *(Extra Credit — +5 pts)*

**Try it yourself:** Use a generative AI tool and include a critical reflection in your deliverable. What did it do well? Where did it fall short? What ethical questions came up for you? Specific examples required.

---

## Academic Integrity

You may use generative AI tools as part of your research — exploring them is encouraged. But all writing, analysis, and recommendations must be your own. Submitting AI-generated text as your own work violates the academic integrity policy and will result in a zero. If you used an AI tool during research, note it in your works cited.
